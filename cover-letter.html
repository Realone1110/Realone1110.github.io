General Faculty Cover Letter

Ridwan O. Olabiyi · Ph.D. Candidate, Industrial Engineering · Arizona State University · Expected Spring 2027

Dear Members of the Faculty Search Committee,

I am writing to express my interest in tenure-track faculty opportunities in Industrial Engineering, Systems Engineering, Manufacturing Engineering, and related areas at the intersection of data science, artificial intelligence, statistics, and computational engineering. I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Industrial Engineering at Arizona State University, advised by Dr. Ashif Iquebal, and I expect to complete my Ph.D. in Spring 2027. My research develops uncertainty-aware scientific machine-learning methods for inverse problems, governing-physics discovery, and computationally efficient modeling of engineering systems. Across this work, I combine Bayesian inference, stochastic modeling, operator learning, causal reasoning, and physics-based simulation to build models that remain useful when measurements are sparse, physical conditions vary, and high-fidelity computation is expensive.

My doctoral research has produced a coherent methodological foundation for an independent faculty program. My Bayesian spatiotemporal inverse heat-conduction work, first published as a peer-reviewed ASME conference paper and later expanded following an editorial recommendation for publication in the ASME Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer, develops a statistically principled approach for recovering hidden physical quantities from sparse and uncertain measurements. I have extended stochastic inverse modeling to elastoplastic material-property characterization from indentation data, with an ongoing journal manuscript involving collaborators at NIST and Oak Ridge National Laboratory. My 2026 Communications Physics article, Discovering Governing Physics in the Presence of Uncertainty, introduces a stochastic inverse framework for discovering governing equations under both measurement noise and intrinsic variability. CRONet, published in Manufacturing Letters, uses operator learning to accelerate repeated finite-element calculations in topology optimization, and related collaborative work on hardware acceleration appeared at IEEE FCCM 2026. I am also developing an image-based 3D reconstruction and quality-assurance system for additively manufactured components that combines multi-view photogrammetry and photometric stereo to reconstruct surface geometry and compare it with CAD or reference models. The longer-term objective is integration with a Meltio robotic metal-additive-manufacturing platform for post-print inspection, in-situ quality monitoring, and targeted surface-processing decisions.

As a faculty member, I plan to build on these demonstrated areas rather than treat future work as separate proposal-like thrusts. My program will center on connected research areas in uncertainty-aware inverse modeling, governing-physics and causal discovery, scientific machine learning for efficient computation, and data-driven quality assurance for engineered systems. Emerging work will connect these areas through adaptive models that can update from new data, recognize when their assumptions are inadequate, and guide the next informative measurement, simulation, inspection, or experiment. Manufacturing and materials characterization will remain important proving grounds, while the methodology is designed to transfer to semiconductor, thermal, energy, and other complex engineered systems.

Teaching and mentoring are central to how I envision this program. My teaching experience spans mathematics, physics, science, and GRE instruction in Nigeria and Industrial Engineering courses at Arizona State University, including Probability and Statistics for Engineering Problem Solving, Quality Control, and Advanced Quality Control. My teaching philosophy begins with purpose: I introduce concepts through the real problems, decisions, or engineering contributions they enable so that students can see why the theory matters before mastering its formal details. Across classroom teaching and GRE mentoring, more than 300 learners have benefited from my instruction. I use authentic examples, transparent expectations, staged projects, reproducible computational workflows, and iterative feedback to help students progress from following procedures to framing and investigating problems independently.

My mentoring spans undergraduate, master's, recent graduate, and doctoral researchers working on image-based 3D reconstruction for additive-manufacturing quality assurance, active learning, generative CAD modeling, inverse modeling for microstructural characterization, and scientific machine learning. I emphasize transferable technical skills, professional development, increasing intellectual ownership, and constructive feedback. I also pay attention to the mental and emotional conditions that affect learning and research productivity, helping students identify appropriate resources and creating an environment in which seeking support is compatible with high expectations. My broader commitment to access includes GRE mentoring, EducationUSA Graduate Boot Camp service in 2021 and 2024, STEM outreach, and sharing preparation resources with students pursuing graduate education.

I value professional service and collaborative problem solving. My service includes leadership in the ASU INFORMS Student Chapter, conference session organization and chairing, journal and conference peer review, educational outreach, and INFORMS volunteering. In 2026, my team (Team RAV) received First Place and the Best Presentation Design Award in the NSF Future Manufacturing Data Challenge and won Problem D in the Semiconductor Solutions Challenge for machine-learning-based static voltage-drop prediction. I was also selected for the 2026 INFORMS Annual Meeting Job Market Showcase and previously named a 2025 Purdue Trailblazers in Engineering Fellow and a 2024 INFORMS QSR Best Student Paper Competition finalist.

I am seeking a faculty environment that values rigorous methodological research, collaborative engineering applications, engaged teaching, and thoughtful mentoring. I would bring a research identity centered on uncertainty-aware scientific machine learning, together with experience spanning inverse problems, physics discovery, operator learning, quality engineering, manufacturing metrology, and student development.

Thank you for your consideration. I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how my research, teaching, mentoring, and service could contribute to your department and its students.

Sincerely,
Ridwan O. Olabiyi
