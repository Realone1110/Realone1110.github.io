Research

Physics-based machine learning for inverse problems and scientific discovery under uncertainty

I develop statistically principled computational methods for recovering hidden physical information from limited and noisy observations. My work spans Bayesian and stochastic inverse modeling, uncertainty quantification, governing-physics discovery, causal scientific discovery, scientific machine learning, and image-based quality assurance for advanced manufacturing.

Research vision

Many engineering problems are inverse in nature: observations are available, while the physical quantities, material properties, governing equations, or mechanisms that generated those observations are only partially known. These problems become particularly difficult when measurements are noisy, parameters vary intrinsically across systems or specimens, forward simulations are computationally expensive, or the assumed governing model is incomplete.

My research addresses this challenge by combining physics-based modeling, statistical inference, uncertainty quantification, and machine learning. A central question across my work is: How can we recover increasingly complex forms of hidden physical information while remaining explicit about what the data and models do not tell us?

Research areas

INVERSE MODELING

Uncertainty-aware inverse modeling

Develop probabilistic methods for recovering latent physical quantities, fields, and distributions of engineering properties from indirect observations while representing measurement noise, physical variability, and other sources of uncertainty.

Representative work: Bayesian spatiotemporal inverse heat conduction and stochastic data-consistent inversion for elastoplastic property characterization from indentation data.

PHYSICS DISCOVERY

Governing-physics discovery under uncertainty

Study how sparse and interpretable governing relationships can be recovered when the governing equation itself is unknown and observations contain both measurement noise and intrinsic system variability.

Representative work: stochastic inverse physics discovery for nonlinear dynamical systems, published in Communications Physics.

SCIENTIFIC ML

Scientific machine learning & computational acceleration

Develop surrogate and operator-learning methods that reduce the cost of high-fidelity simulation and make physically grounded models usable inside repeated design, optimization, inverse, and decision workflows.

Representative work: CRONet for topology optimization and hardware-accelerated scientific ML at IEEE FCCM 2026.

MANUFACTURING VISION

Image-based 3D reconstruction for AM quality assurance

Develop non-contact image-based methods for reconstructing additively manufactured components and measuring surface deviations against CAD or reference geometry. The current approach integrates multi-view photogrammetry for global shape and scale with photometric stereo for high-resolution surface reconstruction under controlled illumination.

Representative research projects

2021–2024

Bayesian spatiotemporal inverse heat conduction

I developed a Bayesian spatiotemporal framework for reconstructing unknown heat flux from noisy temperature measurements. The approach represents the temperature field through spatial, temporal, and interaction structure and uses Bayesian inference to recover the latent boundary quantity while quantifying uncertainty. The work first appeared as a peer-reviewed ASME conference paper in 2023 and, following an editorial recommendation for journal development, was expanded and subsequently published in the ASME Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer in 2024.

2023–Present

Stochastic inverse characterization of elastoplastic properties

This work extends inverse inference from a single unknown quantity to distributions of material properties. The current methodology uses stochastic data-consistent inversion to recover distributions of Young's modulus, yield strength, and strain-hardening behavior from instrumented indentation responses while explicitly accounting for measurement noise and manufacturing-induced variability. The research builds on our 2024 ASME MSEC publication and is being extended in collaboration with researchers at NIST and Oak Ridge National Laboratory.

2023–2026

Discovering governing physics in the presence of uncertainty

I developed a stochastic inverse physics-discovery framework that treats candidate governing-equation coefficients as uncertain quantities and seeks parsimonious models consistent with the distribution of observed system behavior. The framework explicitly addresses both measurement noise and intrinsic system variability and was published in Communications Physics in 2026.

2024–2026

CRONet and accelerated topology optimization

To address expensive repeated simulation, I developed CRONet, a convolutional recurrent operator-approximator network for accelerating topology optimization. The work was published in Manufacturing Letters and later extended through collaborative research on hardware acceleration using AMD Versal AIE-ML engines, published at IEEE FCCM 2026.

Current

Image-based 3D reconstruction and surface quality assurance for robotic AM

I am developing an image-based metrology system for reconstructing additively manufactured components from controlled 2D image sequences and comparing the reconstructed geometry with the original CAD or reference model. The system combines multi-view photogrammetry, which provides global geometry and scale information, with photometric stereo, which uses controlled changes in illumination to recover fine surface detail. The experimental workflow includes programmable lighting and image capture, camera and scene calibration, object extraction, surface-normal and depth reconstruction, point-cloud generation, and multi-view fusion. Current development focuses on robust object extraction, calibration and scale recovery, multi-view fusion, and geometric validation against the reference model. The initial defect-detection scope emphasizes positive surface deviations such as protrusions and excess material. The longer-term objective is integration with a Meltio robotic metal-additive-manufacturing platform to support post-print inspection, in-situ process monitoring for quality control, and location-specific surface-processing decisions.

2025–Present

Causal discovery through counterfactual explanations

My current causal-discovery work investigates how counterfactual explanations and causal diagnostics can help distinguish dynamically meaningful interactions from spurious candidate terms in nonlinear systems. This direction serves as a foundation for a broader research program in causality-aware discovery of governing physics.

Scientific machine learning for efficient engineering modeling

Alongside inverse inference and scientific discovery, I study machine-learning surrogates and operator-learning methods that reduce the cost of repeated high-fidelity computations. This work is motivated by engineering settings in which simulation is accurate but too expensive for design loops, optimization, uncertainty propagation, or real-time decision support.

Surrogate & operator learning

Develop fast approximations of computationally intensive physics models while retaining the structure needed for engineering interpretation and downstream decision making.

Computing-aware scientific ML

Explore how learned engineering models can be mapped to efficient computational architectures, including the hardware-accelerated topology-optimization work published at IEEE FCCM 2026.

Current & emerging directions

My long-term goal is to connect these established research areas into uncertainty-aware, adaptive computational models of physical systems that can update as new information becomes available and recognize when their governing assumptions are incomplete.

ADAPT

Uncertainty-aware digital representations

Combine inverse inference, physics-based simulation, surrogate prediction, and sequential updating as new observations arrive.

DISCOVER

Self-correcting physical models

Develop methods that use discrepancy detection and physics discovery to distinguish parameter drift, sensing error, intrinsic variability, and missing governing structure.

DECIDE

Active sensing and experimentation

Use uncertainty and model inadequacy to identify the next measurement, experiment, or simulation that is most informative for reducing engineering uncertainty.
