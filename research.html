Research

Physics-based machine learning for inverse problems and scientific discovery under uncertainty

I develop statistically principled computational methods for engineering systems in which measurements are sparse or noisy, physical conditions vary, governing structure is incomplete, or repeated high-fidelity simulation is too expensive. My work combines Bayesian and stochastic inverse modeling, uncertainty quantification, governing-physics discovery, causal reasoning, scientific machine learning, and image-based quality assurance.

Research vision

Many engineering problems are inverse in nature: observations are available, while the physical quantities, material properties, governing equations, or mechanisms that generated them are only partially known. These problems become especially difficult when measurements are noisy, parameters vary intrinsically across systems or specimens, forward simulations are computationally expensive, or the assumed governing model is incomplete.

My research asks a central question: How can we infer hidden physical quantities, discover governing structure, and accelerate engineering prediction while rigorously representing stochasticity and uncertainty? I address this question by combining physics-based modeling, statistical inference, uncertainty quantification, causal reasoning, and machine learning.

Research areas

INVERSE MODELING

Uncertainty-aware inverse modeling

I develop probabilistic methods for recovering latent physical quantities, fields, and distributions of engineering properties from indirect observations while separating measurement noise, physical variability, model discrepancy, and other sources of uncertainty.

Representative work: Bayesian spatiotemporal inverse heat conduction and stochastic data-consistent inversion for elastoplastic property characterization from indentation data.

PHYSICS DISCOVERY

Governing-physics discovery under uncertainty

I study how sparse and interpretable governing relationships can be recovered when the governing equation itself is uncertain and observations contain both measurement noise and intrinsic system variability.

Representative work: stochastic inverse physics discovery for nonlinear dynamical systems, published in Communications Physics.

SCIENTIFIC ML

Scientific machine learning & computational acceleration

I develop surrogate and operator-learning methods that reduce the cost of high-fidelity simulation and make physically grounded models usable inside repeated design, optimization, inverse, and decision workflows.

Representative work: CRONet for topology optimization and hardware-accelerated scientific ML at IEEE FCCM 2026.

MANUFACTURING VISION

Image-based 3D reconstruction for AM quality assurance

I develop non-contact image-based methods for reconstructing additively manufactured components and measuring surface deviations against CAD or reference geometry. The current approach integrates multi-view photogrammetry for global shape and scale with photometric stereo for high-resolution surface reconstruction under controlled illumination.

Representative research projects

2021–2024

Bayesian spatiotemporal inverse heat conduction

I developed a Bayesian spatiotemporal framework for reconstructing unknown heat flux from noisy temperature measurements. The approach represents the temperature field through spatial, temporal, and interaction structure and uses Bayesian inference to recover the latent boundary quantity while quantifying uncertainty. The work first appeared as a peer-reviewed ASME conference paper in 2023 and was expanded into the ASME Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer in 2024.

2023–Present

Stochastic inverse characterization of elastoplastic properties

This work extends inverse inference from a single unknown quantity to distributions of material properties. I use stochastic data-consistent inversion to recover distributions of Young's modulus, yield strength, and strain-hardening behavior from instrumented indentation while explicitly accounting for measurement noise and manufacturing-induced variability. The current journal extension is being developed with researchers at NIST and Oak Ridge National Laboratory.

2023–2026

Discovering governing physics in the presence of uncertainty

I developed a stochastic inverse physics-discovery framework that treats candidate governing-equation coefficients probabilistically. Across ten scenarios spanning nonlinear oscillators, chaotic systems, ecological data, and infiltration dynamics, the framework reduced coefficient error by about 82% relative to comparison methods and achieved exact structural identification in seven of ten cases. The work was published in Communications Physics in 2026.

2024–2026

CRONet and accelerated topology optimization

I developed CRONet, a convolutional recurrent operator-approximator network for accelerating topology optimization. On benchmark problems, CRONet reduced computational time by up to 78% while maintaining compliance predictions within 5% of conventional finite-element solutions. The work was published in Manufacturing Letters and later extended through hardware acceleration on AMD Versal AIE-ML engines at IEEE FCCM 2026.

Current

Image-based 3D reconstruction and surface quality assurance for robotic AM

I am developing an image-based metrology system for reconstructing additively manufactured components from controlled 2D image sequences and comparing the reconstructed geometry with CAD or reference models. The system combines multi-view photogrammetry for global geometry and scale with photometric stereo for fine surface reconstruction. Current development focuses on automated imaging, calibration, object extraction, scale recovery, multi-view fusion, and geometric validation. The initial defect-detection scope emphasizes positive surface deviations such as protrusions and excess material. The longer-term objective is integration with a Meltio robotic metal-additive-manufacturing platform for post-print inspection, in-situ process monitoring for quality control, and location-specific surface-processing decisions.

2025–Present

Causal discovery through counterfactual explanations

My current causal-discovery work investigates how counterfactual explanations and complementary causal diagnostics can distinguish dynamically meaningful interactions from spurious candidate terms in nonlinear systems. This direction provides a foundation for broader causality-aware discovery of governing physics.

Scientific machine learning for efficient engineering modeling

Alongside inverse inference and scientific discovery, I study machine-learning surrogates and operator-learning methods that reduce the cost of repeated high-fidelity computations. My goal is not simply to replace simulation with machine learning, but to develop models that indicate when a prediction can be trusted, when additional high-fidelity computation is required, and when new physical data should be collected.

Surrogate & operator learning

I develop fast approximations of computationally intensive physics models while retaining the structure needed for engineering interpretation and downstream decision making.

Computing-aware scientific ML

I explore how learned engineering models can be mapped to efficient computational architectures, including the hardware-accelerated topology-optimization work published at IEEE FCCM 2026.

Current & emerging directions

My future research will connect these established contributions into adaptive engineering models that can update from new data, recognize when their assumptions are inadequate, and determine what measurement, simulation, or experiment should come next.

CHARACTERIZE

Autonomous material characterization

I plan to combine probabilistic inverse inference, physics-based simulation, and multiple sensing modalities to recover spatially varying material properties while distinguishing physical variability from measurement and model uncertainty.

SELF-CORRECT

Physics and causal discovery for self-correcting models

I aim to develop modeling loops in which persistent discrepancy triggers discovery of missing governing structure, followed by model recalibration and targeted experimentation.

TRUST

Trustworthy scientific ML for digital engineering

I will develop fast three-dimensional and multiphysics surrogate models with uncertainty calibration and diagnostics that identify when predictions are reliable, when high-fidelity computation is needed, and when new data should be collected.
