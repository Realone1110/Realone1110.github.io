Research

Uncertainty-aware computational methods for engineering systems

My work develops statistically principled methods for inverse modeling, scientific discovery, and rapid physics-based prediction.

Research program

01

Bayesian inverse modeling

Inverse problems arise when the quantities of engineering interest cannot be measured directly. I develop Bayesian and stochastic methods that recover latent parameters or fields while preserving information about uncertainty, non-uniqueness, and measurement limitations.

02

Physics discovery under uncertainty

My work on stochastic inverse physics discovery addresses governing-equation recovery when systems exhibit both measurement noise and variability in their underlying coefficients.

03

Fast physics-based prediction

I study scientific machine-learning surrogates, including operator-learning methods, that accelerate repeated high-fidelity calculations used in design, optimization, and engineering analysis.

Representative applications

Advanced manufacturing and materials

Applications include elastoplastic property recovery from indentation data, topology optimization, process characterization, and uncertainty-aware engineering prediction.

Thermal and dynamical systems

My research includes Bayesian inverse heat conduction and the discovery of governing equations for nonlinear dynamical systems from uncertain observations.

Future direction

My long-term faculty research agenda is to build uncertainty-aware digital twins that combine inference, physics discovery, rapid surrogate prediction, and active experimentation. The goal is to create engineering models that can update as new data arrive, identify when assumptions fail, and guide the next sensing or experimental decision.
