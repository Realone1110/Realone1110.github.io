Diversity, Mentoring & Inclusive Excellence

Access, belonging, and professional growth through transparent academic practice

My approach to inclusive excellence focuses on the structures that help talented students participate, learn, and contribute when they enter with different preparation, resources, cultural experiences, or familiarity with academic systems.

Make expectations and pathways visible

Academic environments often reward students who already know the unwritten rules: how to approach office hours, read a research paper, ask for feedback, prepare for graduate school, or move from a class project into research. I reduce this hidden curriculum by making expectations explicit through transparent rubrics, staged milestones, worked examples, reproducible workflows, and regular feedback. Students should understand both the technical standard and the process by which they can reach it.

This also shapes how I distribute opportunity within teams. Rotating technical and communication roles helps prevent one student from becoming the permanent programmer, analyst, note-taker, or presenter. As students gain confidence, I transfer more ownership over literature synthesis, model selection, experiment design, validation, and interpretation.

Expand access through teaching, outreach & shared resources

Before beginning my Ph.D., I taught mathematics, physics, science, and advanced-level mathematics in Nigeria and volunteered as a GRE instructor from October 2020 through July 2021. My GRE instruction included quantitative reasoning, verbal reasoning, analytical writing, test-taking strategy, and structured problem solving. Across classroom teaching and GRE mentoring, more than 300 learners have benefited, including students now pursuing Ph.D. and master's degrees and graduates of master's programs.

In 2021, EducationUSA Abuja invited me to deliver a session on GRE and TOEFL/Duolingo test-taking strategies, and I returned as a resource person in 2024. I continue to share study materials, preparation advice, and graduate-school application resources because access to good information can materially change how efficiently students prepare and which opportunities they consider attainable.

Mentor the whole researcher

My mentoring spans undergraduate, master's, recent graduate, and doctoral researchers. I have supported projects in 3D model reconstruction, active learning, generative CAD modeling, inverse modeling for microstructural characterization, and scientific machine learning. I view mentoring as both technical development and professional preparation. Students should leave a research experience with stronger judgment, transferable computational and communication skills, confidence in tackling unfamiliar problems, and a clearer sense of how their work connects to future study or employment.

I also recognize that mental and emotional wellbeing affects learning, creativity, and research output. I aim to create an environment where students can communicate challenges, receive specific and actionable feedback, and be directed to appropriate institutional resources when needs extend beyond my role. High standards and humane mentoring are not competing goals.

Build inclusive research teams and learning communities

In my future group, I plan a tiered mentoring structure in which experienced doctoral researchers help mentor master's and undergraduate students while receiving explicit guidance in supervision and research communication. Students will begin with tractable modules, present results early, and progressively assume intellectual ownership. Remote and hybrid projects can provide additional entry points for students whose schedules, finances, caregiving responsibilities, or institutional location make traditional research participation difficult.

Inclusive excellence also requires continuous self-evaluation. I will use student feedback, mentoring conversations, participation patterns, and learning outcomes to examine whether my course structures and research practices are working as intended and to refine them when they are not.

Commitment as a faculty member

As a faculty member, I would contribute to inclusive excellence through transparent teaching, structured mentoring, accessible research pathways, outreach, resource sharing, and professional development. I want students from varied backgrounds to experience rigor without unnecessary opacity, support without lowered expectations, and increasing ownership as their skills develop.
