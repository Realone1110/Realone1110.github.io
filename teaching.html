Teaching & Mentoring

Connecting theory, data, and engineering decisions

I motivate learning by showing students why a concept matters and how it contributes to solving real problems before moving deeply into the theory. When students understand what knowledge can be used for, it can stimulate curiosity, imagination, active participation, and stronger retention, making abstract ideas easier to connect to practical applications. My teaching and mentoring therefore emphasize real-world context, clear modeling assumptions, physical interpretation, reproducible analysis, and the reasoning behind engineering decisions. I aim to help students progress from solving assigned problems to framing questions, investigating them independently, and transferring what they learn to new settings.

Mentoring philosophy

STRUCTURE

Make expectations explicit

I begin with clear research objectives, intermediate milestones, reproducible workflows, and regular feedback so students understand both the technical goal and a practical path toward independent work.

OWNERSHIP

Build independent judgment

I gradually shift responsibility for literature synthesis, model selection, experiment design, validation, and interpretation to the student, helping them develop confidence, intellectual ownership, and the ability to contribute independently in their field.

TRANSFER

Develop skills beyond one project

I want research training to contribute to each student's professional development. I emphasize transferable skills in problem formulation, computational practice, technical communication, collaboration, and decision making so students are prepared to tackle unfamiliar real-world problems beyond the immediate project.

WELLBEING

Support the whole learner

Learning and research are affected by mental and emotional wellbeing. I try to maintain a respectful environment where students can communicate challenges, receive appropriate support, be directed to relevant resources when needed, and know that their interests and development matter in our interactions.

FEEDBACK

Use feedback for shared growth

I value timely, specific, and constructive feedback that helps students understand how to improve rather than simply what is wrong. I also treat student responses and outcomes as feedback on my own mentoring and teaching, using them to refine how I communicate, scaffold learning, and support independence.

COMMUNICATION

Teach the reasoning, not only the result

I ask students to explain assumptions, defend methodological choices, diagnose failures, and communicate what a result means physically or operationally. The goal is durable understanding rather than completion of a single task.

Teaching principles

CONTEXT

Begin with purpose

Introduce concepts through the physical, societal, or operational problems they help address, then connect that motivation to the statistical, mathematical, or computational theory needed to reason about the problem.

PROCESS

Scaffold reasoning

Help students move from formulation to modeling, computation, validation, and interpretation, with support gradually removed as independence grows.

EVIDENCE

Assess more than the final number

Reward assumptions, model choice, validation, interpretation, reproducibility, and technical communication rather than evaluating only a numerical answer.
