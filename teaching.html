Mentoring philosophy

STRUCTURE

Make expectations explicit

I begin with clear research objectives, intermediate milestones, reproducible workflows, and regular feedback so that students understand both the technical goal and the path toward independent work.

OWNERSHIP

Build independent judgment

I gradually shift responsibility for literature synthesis, model selection, experiment design, validation, and interpretation to the student as their confidence and technical maturity grow.

COMMUNICATION

Teach the reasoning, not only the result

I ask students to explain assumptions, defend methodological choices, diagnose failures, and communicate what a result means physically or operationally.

Teaching principles

CONTEXT

Make data tangible

Start from a physical, manufacturing, or operational decision, then introduce the statistical or computational model needed to reason about it.

PROCESS

Scaffold reasoning

Help students move from formulation to modeling, computation, validation, and interpretation, with support gradually removed as independence grows.

EVIDENCE

Assess more than the final number

Reward assumptions, model choice, validation, interpretation, reproducibility, and technical communication rather than evaluating only a numerical answer.
