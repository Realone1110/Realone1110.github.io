About

I am an Industrial Engineering Ph.D. candidate and Research Associate at Arizona State University. My research asks how engineering systems can be modeled reliably when observations are sparse or noisy, parameters vary across systems or specimens, and the governing physics are only partially known. I develop statistically principled computational methods that combine inference, physical modeling, and machine learning to recover hidden quantities, discover interpretable governing relationships, and accelerate expensive simulation and design workflows.

Research program

INFER

Bayesian & stochastic inverse modeling

Recover latent physical quantities, fields, and material or process parameters from indirect measurements while preserving uncertainty and non-uniqueness.

DISCOVER

Governing physics under uncertainty

Develop interpretable scientific machine-learning methods for discovering dynamical models in the presence of measurement noise and intrinsic variability.

ACCELERATE

Scientific machine learning

Use neural operators, surrogate models, and physics-aware learning to reduce the computational cost of high-fidelity simulation, optimization, and engineering prediction.
