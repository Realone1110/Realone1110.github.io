Research Statement

Uncertainty-Aware Scientific Machine Learning for Engineering Systems

I develop computational methods for engineering systems in which measurements are sparse or noisy, physical conditions vary, governing structure is only partially known, or high-fidelity simulation is too expensive for repeated use. My research combines inverse modeling, uncertainty quantification, scientific machine learning, and physics discovery to infer hidden physical information and build interpretable, efficient models.

Research foundation

INFER

Uncertainty-aware inverse modeling

My Bayesian spatiotemporal inverse heat-conduction work reconstructs latent thermal quantities from sparse measurements while quantifying uncertainty. I extend this principle to stochastic data-consistent characterization of elastoplastic property distributions from indentation data, including measurement noise and manufacturing-induced variability.

DISCOVER

Governing physics under uncertainty

My 2026 Communications Physics work formulates governing-equation discovery as a stochastic inverse problem. Current research extends this direction through counterfactual explanations and causal diagnostics for distinguishing dynamically meaningful interactions from spurious candidate terms.

ACCELERATE

Scientific machine learning

CRONet uses operator learning to accelerate topology optimization, with subsequent collaborative work on hardware acceleration. I also develop image-based 3D reconstruction and surface inspection methods for additive-manufacturing quality assurance using photogrammetry, photometric stereo, and reference-geometry comparison.

Research trajectory

These areas form a connected program rather than separate methodological projects. Inverse modeling addresses hidden physical quantities and property distributions. Physics and causal discovery address cases in which the governing model itself is uncertain. Scientific machine learning reduces the computational burden needed to use these models in repeated engineering workflows. Manufacturing and materials systems provide important testbeds, while the underlying methods are relevant to thermal, semiconductor, energy, and other engineered systems.

My emerging work connects these capabilities through adaptive computational models that can update as observations arrive, diagnose when existing assumptions are inadequate, and use uncertainty to guide the next informative measurement, simulation, inspection, or experiment. This creates opportunities in uncertainty-aware digital representations, model discrepancy and self-correction, and active sensing and experimentation.

Research culture & impact

I aim to build a collaborative research group in which reproducibility, interpretation, and student development are part of the technical infrastructure. Version-controlled software, documented benchmarks, reproducible workflows, and progressive student ownership will support research that is both auditable and extensible. Existing collaborations with researchers at NIST and Oak Ridge National Laboratory provide a foundation for broader interdisciplinary partnerships.

The long-term objective is to develop engineering models that remain useful because they can represent what is uncertain, recognize when assumptions fail, identify missing physical structure, and update predictions or decisions as new information becomes available.
