Research Statement

Uncertainty-Aware Scientific Machine Learning for Engineering Systems

Modern engineering increasingly depends on computational models that must remain reliable under stochastic variability, sparse or noisy measurements, incomplete physical information, and the high cost of repeated high-fidelity simulation. My research asks a central question: How can we infer hidden physical quantities, discover governing structure, and accelerate engineering prediction while rigorously representing stochasticity and uncertainty?

I address this question through Bayesian inverse modeling, stochastic physics discovery, causal reasoning, and scientific machine learning, with applications in manufacturing, materials, thermal, semiconductor, and other engineered systems.

Previous contributions

INFER

Uncertainty-aware inverse modeling

I developed a Bayesian spatiotemporal framework for reconstructing unobserved thermal quantities from sparse measurements while quantifying uncertainty. I extended the same principle to stochastic data-consistent inversion of elastoplastic property distributions from indentation data, explicitly representing measurement noise and manufacturing-induced variability.

Foundation: peer-reviewed ASME conference work, a 2024 Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer article, and an ongoing journal extension with NIST and Oak Ridge National Laboratory collaborators.

DISCOVER

Physics discovery under uncertainty

I developed a stochastic inverse framework for discovering governing equations when the model form itself is uncertain. Across ten nonlinear, chaotic, ecological, and infiltration scenarios, the framework reduced coefficient error by about 82% relative to comparison methods and achieved exact structural identification in seven of ten cases.

Foundation: published in Communications Physics in 2026, with complementary work on counterfactual explanations and causal evidence.

ACCELERATE

Scientific machine learning

I developed CRONet, a convolutional recurrent operator-approximator network for topology optimization. On benchmark problems, CRONet reduced computational time by up to 78% while maintaining compliance predictions within 5% of conventional finite-element solutions. The work was later extended through hardware acceleration on AMD Versal AIE-ML engines.

Foundation: Manufacturing Letters and IEEE FCCM 2026.

Future research & opportunities

My future research will connect these contributions into adaptive engineering models that can update from new data, recognize when their assumptions are inadequate, and determine what measurement, simulation, or experiment should come next.

CHARACTERIZE

Uncertainty-aware inverse modeling for autonomous characterization

I will develop probabilistic inverse methods that distinguish intrinsic physical variability from measurement noise, numerical approximation, surrogate error, and model discrepancy. Autonomous material characterization provides an early testbed in which indentation, thermal, image, and other sensing modalities can be combined with physics-based simulation to infer spatially varying material properties and their uncertainty.

SELF-CORRECT

Physics and causal discovery for self-correcting models

I will extend stochastic physics discovery to partially known models, heterogeneous observations, and changing operating conditions. The longer-term objective is a self-correcting loop in which persistent model discrepancy triggers discovery of missing governing structure, followed by recalibration and targeted experimentation.

TRUST

Trustworthy scientific machine learning for digital engineering

Building on CRONet, I will develop fast surrogate and operator-learning models for three-dimensional and multiphysics problems while incorporating uncertainty calibration and diagnostics for predictions outside a reliable operating regime. Applications include topology optimization, manufacturing process models, and image-based geometric inspection.

A connected research program

These directions form a coherent program: inverse modeling recovers hidden states and properties, physics and causal discovery identify missing structure, and scientific machine learning makes repeated inference and engineering decision making computationally practical. Manufacturing and materials provide natural proving grounds, while the methods extend to thermal, semiconductor, energy, and other complex engineered systems.

My existing collaborations with NIST and Oak Ridge National Laboratory provide a foundation for external validation and interdisciplinary partnerships. I plan to build a research group centered on reproducible software, documented benchmarks, FAIR data practices, and progressive student ownership of methodological questions.

Ultimately, my goal is to develop engineering models that remain useful not because they are assumed to be correct, but because they can quantify uncertainty, recognize when their assumptions fail, and update their predictions and decisions accordingly.
