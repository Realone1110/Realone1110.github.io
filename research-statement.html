Research Statement

Uncertainty-Aware Scientific Machine Learning for Engineering Systems

Modern engineering increasingly depends on computational models that must remain trustworthy when physical conditions vary, measurements are noisy or incomplete, and high-fidelity simulation is too expensive for repeated decision making. My research asks how models can infer hidden physical quantities, discover governing structure, and support efficient engineering prediction while representing uncertainty honestly.

Research foundation

Bayesian and stochastic inverse modeling

My work on the inverse heat-conduction problem developed a Bayesian spatiotemporal approach that integrates heat-transfer structure with statistical inference to recover unobserved thermal quantities from sparse measurements while quantifying uncertainty. The work first appeared as a peer-reviewed ASME conference paper in 2023 and, following an editorial recommendation for journal development, was expanded and published in the ASME Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer in 2024. I have extended the same principle to instrumented indentation, where stochastic data-consistent inversion is used to recover distributions of elastoplastic properties while accounting for measurement noise and manufacturing-induced variability. The current extension is being developed with collaborators at NIST and Oak Ridge National Laboratory.

Physics discovery under uncertainty

To address model-form uncertainty, I developed the stochastic inverse physics-discovery framework reported in Communications Physics in 2026. The method treats unknown coefficients in candidate governing equations as random variables and infers distributions consistent with observed system behavior. Across ten scenarios spanning nonlinear oscillators, chaotic systems, ecological data, and infiltration dynamics, the framework reduced coefficient error by about 80% relative to seven comparison methods and achieved exact structural identification in seven of ten cases. A complementary direction investigates counterfactual explanations and causal diagnostics for distinguishing dynamically meaningful interactions from spurious candidate terms.

Scientific machine learning for efficient computation

My CRONet work addresses the computational cost of repeated simulation using a convolutional recurrent operator-approximator network for topology optimization. Published in Manufacturing Letters, CRONet reduces the burden of repeated finite-element calculations while preserving close agreement with benchmark solutions. The work was later extended through hardware acceleration using AMD Versal AIE-ML engines at IEEE FCCM 2026. I also work on 3D model reconstruction and related engineering machine-learning problems that connect learned representations with geometric modeling and design workflows.

Research areas

Uncertainty-aware inverse modeling

Probabilistic recovery of latent states, parameters, and distributions from indirect and heterogeneous observations while separating physical variability from measurement and model uncertainty.

Governing-physics and causal discovery

Discovery of sparse, interpretable governing relationships from uncertain data, with counterfactual and causal diagnostics for assessing physical meaning.

Scientific machine learning and computational acceleration

Surrogate and operator-learning methods for reducing high-fidelity computational cost while retaining diagnostics for trust, correction, and out-of-distribution use.

Current & emerging directions

My future work will connect these research areas into adaptive models that can update from new data, recognize when their assumptions are inadequate, and guide the next informative measurement, simulation, or experiment.

Uncertainty-aware digital representations

Combine inverse inference, physics-based simulation, surrogate prediction, and sequential updating as new observations arrive.

Self-correcting physical models

Use discrepancy detection and physics discovery to distinguish parameter drift, sensing error, intrinsic variability, and missing governing structure.

Active sensing and experimentation

Connect uncertainty to value-of-information criteria so new data are collected where they are most useful for model discrimination and engineering decisions.

Research culture, collaboration & impact

I plan to build a research group in which reproducibility and mentorship are treated as technical infrastructure. Version-controlled software, documented benchmarks, reproducible computational workflows, and FAIR data practices will make results auditable and lower the barrier for new students to contribute. Existing collaborations with NIST and Oak Ridge National Laboratory provide a foundation for national-laboratory partnerships, while manufacturing, semiconductor, materials, thermal, and energy applications create opportunities for interdisciplinary and industry collaboration.

The broader vision is a class of engineering models that remain useful not because they are assumed to be correct, but because they can quantify what they do not know, identify when their assumptions fail, learn what physics is missing, and update predictions or decisions accordingly.
